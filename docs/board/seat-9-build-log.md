## 1. Phase 0 — frame

### 1.1 Accomplishment, purpose, typical performer

**Board:** predicts which real-room geometry and material choices change what people hear; validates presets and the material library. For: critique, advise. Typical performer: not given by the Board for this seat.

**Amended accomplishment (adopted):** *From a description of a real room (type, size, shape, surfaces, occupancy), predicts which geometric and material choices a target listener can hear at a given position, by how much and in which direction, before anything is built or rendered; states the uncertainty of that prediction; and validates presets and material data against measured rooms and blind listening rather than against catalogue numbers or reputation.* The amendment adds three things the exemplars share and the Board's wording lacks: the direction and size of the predicted change (not just "changes"), the uncertainty, and validation against measurement.

**For:** critique and advise (Seat 2's engine assumptions, Seat 6's exposed controls, your preset and material library). It decides nothing on its own; it hands Seat 1 the audibility claims to test.

**Typical performer (researched, see 2.4):** designs and judges by reverberation time against a volume-based target (DIN 18041 and its national siblings), predicts RT with a statistical formula fed by manufacturer absorption tables taken at face value, treats early reflections as a nuisance to absorb at "reflection points", validates by sighted listening and by whether the RT target was hit, and attributes complaints to taste.

### 1.2 Measure of excellence, typical vs exemplary, PIP

The accomplishment has two measurable faces:

1. **Prediction accuracy.** Of the design claims the acoustician makes before a room exists ("this change will be audible as X"), the share confirmed afterwards by measurement and blind listening. No published base rate exists for this; in the hall record the proxy is "first-time-right": halls that opened without needing major acoustic remediation. Post-war examples of misses are many and documented (Royal Festival Hall 1951, Arie Crown 1960, Philharmonic Hall 1962, Sydney Opera House 1973, Barbican 1982, Avery Fisher 1976). `[Obs, Peer; H]`
2. **Scored listening.** Where a laboratory paradigm exists (Lokki's loudspeaker orchestra; Seat 1's plausibility tests), the measure is the match between the acoustician's predicted ranking of rooms or changes and the blind-test ranking.

**Typical level:** RT within ±10–15% of target (Harvie-Clark et al. report RT prediction is unreliable enough that designers over-design or risk test failure `[Peer; H]`); audibility of changes never tested; perceptual outcome unknown until complaints arrive.

**Exemplary level:** predictions of *which* feature will be heard, confirmed later (Beranek's early-reflection insight at Arie Crown and its confirmation across 54 halls; Marshall's lateral-reflection hypothesis built into Christchurch 1972 and acclaimed; Lokki's shoebox-dynamics prediction confirmed in blind tests; D'Antonio's measured diffusion behaving as predicted). Toyota's 32 major halls with a majority praised. `[Obs, Peer; H]`

**PIP (Gilbert):** no numerical base rate exists. My estimate: exemplars are roughly 3× more likely than typical practice to be first-time-right on the perceptual outcome, and the gap is wider on *which detail matters* than on hitting an RT number. `[Hyp; L]` This is the quantity Phase 4's back-test probes.

### 1.3 Skill–luck placement

- **Persistence.** Toyota's praised halls run from Suntory (1986) through Disney (2003), Copenhagen (2009), Elbphilharmonie (2017) and Pierre Boulez Saal (2017): persistence over 30 years is a skill signal. But his record also includes two halls (Helsinki 2011, Elbphilharmonie 2017) rated low or criticised by listeners and scientists. `[Obs, Peer; H]` Beranek: one famous failure (1962), later successes (TOC 1997, NNT opera 1997). Griesinger: LARES in hundreds of installations with one documented over-tuning (Carmel 2003). D'Antonio: forty years of products that measure as predicted. Lokki: results replicated across six halls and multiple listener groups.
- **Correlation of decision with result.** Weak at the hall scale: Philharmonic Hall's decisive variable (seat count and shape) was imposed on the acoustician. The denominator question (how many fan-shaped halls by competent consultants disappointed?) has a long list of yeses (Mann Auditorium 1957, Alberta Jubilee 1957, Beethovenhalle 1959, Arie Crown 1960, all cited by Beranek). `[SR-i post; H]`
- **Reversion to the mean.** Opening-night verdicts revert both ways: Suntory's musicians hated it for 3–4 years then stopped complaining with nothing changed (Toyota); Elbphilharmonie's acclaim gave way to criticism within two years. Hall verdicts therefore need ≥2 years and multiple sources before they count as outcomes. `[SR-i post; M]`
- **Trials needed.** Hall-level: a record of ≥5 completed halls with pre-opening predictions on paper, plus ≥2 years of reception each, before "skill" can be read. Laboratory-level: each blind test is a trial; Lokki's dozen-plus studies make his claims the most skill-attributable on the slate. For *this product*, the kind trial exists and is cheap: a preset or material claim can be scored against a measured room in days. The persona is built to prefer that trial.
- **Placement:** hall design sits mid-continuum, luck-heavy per trial; room-acoustic *prediction of audible change* sits toward skill when it is tested blind against measurement, which is the regime the persona operates in.

### 1.4 Context of operation

Inside your plugin project: advising Seat 2 on what the engine must get right and may skip; advising Seat 6 on which room and material controls to expose; owning the material library (names, bands, coefficients, uncertainty, scattering, occupancy) and the preset library (room types with target parameter ranges and a measured reference each). No lab, no scale models, no client; cheap compute, published data, and your own blind-test capacity (unknown, `[PRD-dependent]`).

### 1.5 Kind or wicked, task by task

| Recurring task | Environment | Why | Persona mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Predict RT/EDT/G from volume and absorption | Kind physics, **wicked inputs** | Sabine/Eyring are reliable when absorption is right; published coefficients disagree between labs and exceed 1.0 (Vercammen; Scrosati et al.) | Compute, then bracket with coefficient uncertainty; verify against a measured reference |

| Diagnose a complaint about a finished room/preset | Mixed | Measurable, but perception is noisy and adapts (Toyota's Suntory story) | Measure first; separate adaptation from room |

### 1.6 Contributory vs language-shaped

**Contributory (beyond this persona):** listening in real rooms; measuring impulse responses; building or reading scale models; running listening panels; hearing a preset. **Language-shaped (in reach):** deciding which physical variables deserve a control; predicting direction and size of a change from physics and the exemplars' cases; designing the validation protocol; auditing coefficient data; critiquing presets against measured references; writing the acceptance criteria Seat 1 will test. The persona must say, each time it reaches a contributory step: *here is the measurement or blind test I need from you, and here is how to run it cheaply.*

### 1.7 Premortem (why this model gives bad advice in a year)

1. It imports concert-hall judgement into bedroom-sized rooms where different physics dominates (modes, near-field, direct sound) and the exemplar set is thin on small rooms. *Mitigation:* explicit small-room rules from D'Antonio and the Toole/Olive literature; an open question for a small-room exemplar.
2. It treats manufacturer coefficient tables as truth. *Mitigation:* the coefficient audit procedure and uncertainty bands.
3. It universalises one exemplar's contested stance (Griesinger's "early reflections are detrimental" or Lokki's "lateral reflections are the secret"). *Mitigation:* contested-zone markings; context tests (room size, position, task).
4. It argues for physical completeness (diffraction, modes everywhere) against the shared frame. *Mitigation:* every recommendation passes a JND gate and goes to Seat 1.
5. It judges presets without a measured reference. *Mitigation:* no preset is "validated" without a named measured room and a blind A/B.
6. It forgets occupancy. *Mitigation:* people are a material; audience/floor area is the dominant absorber in large rooms (Beranek).
7. It drifts into architecture (seat counts, sightlines) that a plugin user never faces. *Mitigation:* scope section and drift test.

### 2.4 Typical-performer profile (from the same kinds of sources)

- **Design criterion:** RT versus room volume, from DIN 18041 or a national equivalent; "the way a room sounds is primarily determined by its reverberation time" is the espoused theory of a typical vendor page `[SR-g; H]`. National standards in >50 countries use RT as the main criterion (Tardini et al. 2025) `[Peer; H]`.
- **Prediction method:** statistical formula plus catalogue coefficients; RT prediction is unreliable enough that consultants over-design or wave their arms at test failure (Harvie-Clark et al., IOA) `[Peer; H]`; absorption concentrated on one surface breaks the diffuse-field assumption `[Peer; H]`.
- **Material data:** taken from manufacturers, who shop for the lab with the highest coefficient (Vercammen) `[Peer; H]`; coefficients above 1.0 accepted as "edge effect" without understanding `[Peer; H]`.
- **Treatment doctrine:** absorb first reflection points; LEDE/RFZ applied by rote in home rooms although blind tests found no main effect of treatment on mixing performance (King, Leonard and Sikora, AES) `[Peer; H]`.
- **Validation:** sighted; by RT compliance; complaints handled as taste or as "musicians need time" without separating adaptation from fault.
- **Historical instance:** Royal Festival Hall 1951, where "the world's experts" misdiagnosed the low RT as ceiling absorption instead of underestimated audience absorption, and the error propagated to four more halls (Beranek 1988) `[SR-i post; H]`.

## 3. Phase 2 — research trail

Each incident: timeline → decision points → first-noticed cue → expectancy → source and independence. War stories told to peers are marked **(peers)**; incidents told to interviewers **(interview)**.

### 3.1 Leo Beranek (10 incidents)

**B1. Royal Festival Hall diagnosis chain, 1951–1959 (peers).** RFH opened with RT <1.5 s occupied against a 1.7 s design; consultants blamed an absorbent ceiling; the misdiagnosis propagated to Mann (1957), Alberta Jubilee (1957), Beethovenhalle (1959). Beranek assembled drawings and measured RTs of >40 halls and found audience absorption scales with floor area occupied, nearly independent of head count (1959/1960). Decision point: trust the per-person rule or rebuild it from data; he rebuilt it. Cue: a pattern of misses across halls, not one hall. Source: Beranek, IOA 1988 "25 years" `[SR-i post; H]`; statistical confirmation Ramakrishna 1979 `[Peer; H]`; "absorption per person had been assumed about half of what later studies found" `[SR-i post; H]`.

**B2. Tanglewood Music Shed canopy, 1959 (peers).** 5,000-seat shed, RT ~2 s, complaints about hearing soloists and players hearing each other. BBN added diffusing walls around the orchestra and an irregular overhead canopy of non-planar triangular panels (2.5–8 m, array ~50% open). Hailed by Stern, Ormandy, NYT, Globe. Expectancy formed: canopies work. Beranek later says the success "has been difficult to duplicate in other types of halls". Source: IOA 1988 `[SR-i post; H]`; outcome `[Peer post; H]`.

**B3. Arie Crown Theatre, Chicago, 1960 — failure that produced the insight (peers).** First concert: "dead", orchestra "remote". Next day he sat alone in the empty hall and compared it with Boston Symphony Hall and Tanglewood: RT could not be the cause (1.7 vs 1.8 s), nor size (Tanglewood also 5,000). The one difference: fan shape, so no early reflections; in Boston balcony fronts return lateral reflections within ~20 ms at row 15, in Tanglewood the canopy does. He named the gap the initial-time-delay gap. Decision point: look for a new variable rather than defend RT. Source: IOA 1988 `[SR-i post; H]`.

**B4. The 54-hall study and *Music, Acoustics and Architecture*, 1962 (peers, published pre-Philharmonic outcome).** Interviewed ~50 conductors and critics; ITDG correlated best with ratings, then mid-frequency RT and bass ratio; intimacy weighted ~40% in his scheme. Source: IOA 1988 `[SR-i post; H]`; book itself is `pre` for Philharmonic Hall `[Obs pre; H]`. Method caveat: expert interviews are halo-exposed.

**B5. Philharmonic Hall, Lincoln Center, 1959–1963 — the failure (contemporaneous and post).** May 1959: 2,400-seat shoebox modelled on Boston. Press attacked the seat count; Lincoln Center ordered ≥200 more seats over his objections with foundations being poured; Abramovitz relaxed the side walls (fan-shaped at the rear), sloped the side tiers down, final 2,646 seats. Wooden wall diffusers were cut for budget. Opening Sept 1962: weak bass, echo, poor stage hearing. Measured RT with simulated audience 3.0/2.6/2.2/2.2/2.1/1.6 s (125–4k) yet the hall sounded dry: fan shape weakens the reverberant field relative to direct sound. Clouds too small and regular to reflect bass; <50% open area. Committee (Knudsen, Veneklasen, Keilholz, Schroeder) adopted a plan at odds with his; he disavowed the remediation on 18 Sept 1963. Keilholz removed the clouds 1969; Harris gutted the hall 1976; Akustiks rebuilt it 2022 (2,200 seats, stage pushed 25 ft out, volume increased, 1:20 model). Decision points: (a) stay or resign when the seat count rose — stayed; (b) cloud geometry; (c) disavow the committee — did. Cues he noticed afterwards: bass weakness, side walls "too far apart to provide early reflections to the center seats". Sources: Scarbrough 2023 `[Peer post; H]`; Beranek 1963 letter via Scarbrough `[Obs pre-remediation; H]`; Beranek, Physics Today 1986 `[SR-i post; M]`; Barron's analysis `[Peer post; H]`.

**B6. Seat-dip effect discovered during the investigation, 1964 (peers, contemporaneous).** Schultz and Watters (BBN) and Sessler and West (Bell Labs) independently quantified the low-frequency attenuation of sound grazing over seating (~100–300 Hz; dip near a quarter-wavelength of seat height); Schroeder noted it is usually masked by LF energy in early overhead reflections; raking ≥15° eliminates it (West and Sessler 1966). Source: Davies IOA; Knatt et al. 2017; Kahle Acoustics 2025 `[Peer; H]`. Modern caveat: Kahle et al. 2025 argue the grazing attenuation is broadband and the narrow dip is the smaller problem `[Peer; M]`.

**B7. Tokyo Opera City concert hall, 1997 (peers).** 1,632 seats, 15,300 m³, occupied RT 1.95 s; distorted pyramid ceiling peaking 28 m; QRD diffusers on the long ceiling to kill an echo and strengthen lateral reflections; CAD plus a 1:10 wooden model, whose results changed balcony slopes; a year of model testing before construction; "performers and critics judge the acoustics excellent." Decision point: accept an unprecedented geometry because the model and the lateral-reflection analysis said it would work. Source: Hidaka, Beranek et al., JASA 2000 `[SR-i post, with pre-opening model data; H]`; Beranek, "Tale of two halls" `[SR-i post; M]`.

**B8. New National Theatre opera house, Tokyo, 1997 (peers).** Surfaces, balcony faces and shaped ceiling distribute singers' voices uniformly at levels that override the pit. Source: Beranek, Hidaka, Masuda JASA 2000 `[SR-i post; M]`.

**B9. Self-correction on ITDG and intimacy, 2004–2008 (peers).** In JASA 2004 he acknowledged intimacy is multisensory and the word choice "may have been unfortunate"; told Hyde and Kahle that ITDG matters mainly because a long gap shortens the window for reflections before 80 ms; in JAES 2008 identified EDT as the metric best correlated with preference. Source: Hyde, Acoustics 2019; Kahle et al. IOA; AA2018 programme `[SR-g post; H]`.

**B10. Sabine vs Eyring and audience/chair absorption, 2006 (peers).** Derived audience and chair coefficients from 20 built halls; for a postulated occupied audience area and RT, hall volume is proportional to the audience absorption coefficient, which depends on chair upholstery and hall shape; residual (non-audience) areas treated separately. Source: Beranek JASA 2006 `[Obs; H]`.

**Failure/near-miss:** B3, B5. **War stories to peers:** B1–B3, B9, B10. **Interview/trade:** B7's SVC piece.

Toyota in the model photo. Source: Lokki, IOA 2015; FMQ 2019 `[Peer post, scored; H]`.

**T5. Philharmonie de Paris, 2015 (peers).** Marshall Day (Harold Marshall) led the acoustic design; Toyota consulted for Nouvel and supervised the 1:10 model (6×5×3 m). Source: 99% Invisible; Marshall and Day IOA 2015; Bachtrack `[Peer post; M]`.

**T6. Elbphilharmonie, 2017 (interview, peers, press).** ~2,100 seats, tall vineyard. "White skin": ~10,000 unique CNC-milled gypsum-fibre panels generated by Benjamin Koren's algorithm from Toyota's requirements (broad surfaces, deeper relief at the back, "only the acoustical conditions, like the size of roughness or irregularity"). Reception: Washington Post "clarity, precision and cool objectivity"; Time 2018 praise; Jan 2019 audience shouting "can't hear you" at Kaufmann behind-stage balance problems; Muti called the acoustics mediocre; management replied the hall is "not for beginners". Decision point: let architecture generate the surface within acoustic constraints. Source: Bachtrack; Inverse; Slipped Disc; Wikipedia with refs; Lokki FMQ `[SR-g post; Peer post; H]`.

**T7. Pierre Boulez Saal, Berlin, 2017 (interview).** Small hall cited as "a marvel of intimacy". Source: Bachtrack `[SR-g post; L]`.

**T8. Method statements (interview, general).** Clients ask only for "a good hall"; site constraints dominate; "I can only do the things I know how to do"; above 2,000 seats design "gets extremely difficult"; computers since 1995 made reflection patterns calculable; "we didn't believe acoustics can be explained only by the numbers", hence the model; sceptical of adjustable and multipurpose halls; materials "not significant", roughness is. ### 3.3 David Griesinger (9 incidents; hall and perception set only, Lexicon reverb design left to Seat 3)

**G1. LARES, 1988–1989, Elgin Theatre Toronto (peers).** With Steve Barbar, at the request of acousticians Muncy and Tanner. Problem: electroacoustic enhancement coloured and fed back. Decision: decorrelate microphone and loudspeaker signals in real time with patented time-variant processing; add digital delay so enhanced sound arrives with or just after the direct sound. First installation: 2 microphones at the balcony edge, 116 loudspeaker channels; a lighting truss not in the drawings obstructed mic placement. AES 1991 paper; US patent 5,109,419 (1992); ASA Sabine Medal for the perceptual body of work. Source: Wikipedia LARES with primary refs; ecousticsystems.com history; proav.de `[SR-i post; Peer; H]`.

**G2. Sunset Center, Carmel, 2003 — near-miss (press).** After installation the acoustic signature was "too long and too detailed"; coughs audible on stage; reverberation likened to the Grand Canyon; Barbar returned for ten more days of tuning (city paid $10,000); retuned with festival management, favourable result. Cue: audience noise exposed by enhancement. Source: Wikipedia LARES with press refs `[Peer post; M]`.

**G3. Jay Pritzker Pavilion, 2004 (press).** First permanent outdoor LARES in the US, on Gehry's trellis for 11,000 listeners; radio engineer praised projection over distance. Source: same `[Peer post; M]`.

**G4. Spaciousness theory, 1997 (peers).** Acustica paper separating early spatial impression from late envelopment; criticism of IACC/LF as sufficient measures. Source: his site; Sengpiel index `[Obs; M]`.

**G5. D/R experiments with image-source binaural models (peers).** Varied the direct component at a typical seat; subjects reported localisation and spaciousness; concluded few seats in a hall have D/R in the favourable range; critical distance in a shoebox ~7 m. Source: IOA, "The importance of the direct to reverberant ratio" `[Obs; M]`.

**G6. LOC and proximity, 2011–2018 (peers).** Proximity depends on detecting the direct sound via phase regularity of harmonics above ~1.5 kHz; reflections from any angle scramble it; EMPAC experiments: reflections within 5 ms increase proximity, after 7 ms decrease it; he corrected his public LOC code with a 6 ms crossfade after a user found an error; Neil and Vigeant later found proximity dominated preference across many halls, with "no measure for proximity" — he pointed to LOC. Source: davidgriesinger.com; IOA 2015, 2018 `[SR-i; Obs; H]`.

**G7. Williams Hall, New England Conservatory (peers, BAS talk 2010).** A curtain drawn across the rear of the stage removed early reflections that had been compromising the direct sound and D/R — a cheap in-situ test. Source: Boston Audio Society report `[SR-i post; M]`.

**G8. Claims in his 2010 DC AES talk (peers, general).** Reflections 10–100 ms reduce clarity, engagement and envelopment; hall shape does not scale (a 2,000-seat design halved has fewer good seats); optimum D/R above 700 Hz is higher in smaller halls; small halls need absorption or diffusion of early reflections more than large halls; current measurements ignore D/R700+ and the direct-to-reverberation-buildup delay. Source: AES DC section report `[SR-g; M]`.

**G9. Rear-seat lateral reflections detrimental (peers).** In a measured hall, deleting early lateral reflections from rear-half impulse responses improved proximity and envelopment with no loudness loss. Source: IOA 2015 `[Obs; M]`. **Contested** against Marshall, Barron, Lokki (see validity map).

**Failure/near-miss:** G2; the LOC code error (G6). **Non-practice advantages:** Lexicon's platform for LARES; later none — self-funded probe microphones, binaural rigs and a website. Portable to you: yes.

### 3.4 Peter D'Antonio with Trevor Cox (10 incidents)

**D1. Underground Sound, late 1970s–1983 (interview).** Diffraction physicist at NRL; no literature on control-room physics; built a LEDE room as published and was disappointed: strong specular reflections interfering with the direct sound. Decision: "come down from Mars" and import the concert-hall notion of an initial time delay into a small room. Source: Mix Oct 2023 `[SR-i post; H]`.

**D2. RFZ, 1984 (interview, peers).** Splayed side walls and a slanted ceiling send first reflections past the mix position to the rear; rear-wall diffusers return energy spread over a semicircle; reflective side walls return diffused energy at ~55°; "passive surround". First industry article Mix Aug 1984; AES 1984. Source: Mix 2023; audiotechnology.com `[SR-i post; H]`.

**D3. Schroeder diffusers as 2-D crystals, 1980–1983 (peers).** Physics Today Oct 1980 article; recognised reflection phase gratings as two-dimensional versions of periodic crystal lattices; AES 74th convention Oct 1983, with Schroeder as lead speaker; founded RPG Dec 1983. Source: Springer chapter; AES paper record `[SR-i post; H]`.

**D4. First measurement, Syn-Aud-Con Dallas (interview).** Two four-foot QRDs shipped to Dallas; TEF analyser showed the time response — "that's what they should do". Don Davis, Bob Todrank befriended him; Oak Ridge Boys' studio (1984) first commercial installation; Dallas Sound Labs with Russ Berger. Source: Mix 2023 `[SR-i post; H]`.

**D5. The "midrange speaker" problem (interview).** Original RPG acted like a midrange device; developed HF, MF and LF diffusers; QRD 734 patent 1987 as the first commercial diffuser. Source: Mix 2023 `[SR-i post; H]`.

**D6. From gyms to the goniometer to ISO 17497-2, 1991–2012 (interview, peers).** Felt an urgency to verify predictions experimentally; measured in sports facilities and his son's gymnasium, then built a scale-model goniometer; proposed a diffusion coefficient in 1991; chaired the AES working group that produced AES-4id-2001; ISO 17497-2 published 2012. Source: Mix 2023; RPG history `[SR-i post; H]`.

**D7. Cox's BEM optimisation, 1995 (peers).** D'Antonio refereed Cox's JASA paper on optimised profiled diffusers using boundary-element prediction; better, more uniform diffusion than Schroeder designs; led to three co-authored books. Source: Salford repository; Mix 2023 `[Obs; Peer; H]`.

**D8. Cox, Inchindown oil tanks, 2014 (peers).** Starting pistol per ISO 3382; Guinness's SPL-meter definition gave 75 s; octave-band T30 at 125 Hz exceeded 112 s; he argued the scientific definition should govern. A clean case of "which measure counts". Source: Cox and Kilpatrick, JASA 2015 letter; NPR `[SR-i post; H]`.

**D9. Absorption coefficient reproducibility, 2010–2020 (peers).** D'Antonio et al. ASA 2011 "state of the art in measurement of acoustical coefficients"; Vercammen's round robins: spread far worse than design, control or law can accept; coefficients >1.0; edge effect; suppliers shop for high-reading labs; ISO 354 revised 2018 with partial improvement; Scrosati et al. 2020 inter-laboratory test. Source: Vercammen ISRA 2010, ICA 2019; Scrosati 2020 `[Peer; H]`.

**D10. Sale and relaunch, 2016–2017 (interview).** Sold RPG Diffusor Systems 2016; founded RPG Acoustical Systems 2017 with an Acoustical Research Center (285 m³ reverberation room, goniometer, 25-ft impedance tube); Sabine Medal 2023. Source: Mix 2023 `[SR-i post; M]`.

**Failure/near-miss:** D1, D5. **Non-practice advantages:** NRL training in diffraction physics (not portable); a carport and plywood (portable).

### 3.5 Tapio Lokki (9 incidents)

**L1. Building the loudspeaker orchestra, 2008–2011 (peers).** 33–34 calibrated loudspeakers in orchestral positions on stage, anechoic single-instrument recordings, a 6-microphone array receiver, the Spatial Decomposition Method to reproduce measured halls over a 3-D loudspeaker array; Pätynen's 2011 thesis. Funded by an ERC Starting Grant (~€880k). Decision: eliminate performance variability so halls can be compared blind at identical seats. Source: Pätynen thesis; JASA 2012; ERC story `[Obs; H]`.

**L2. Temporal envelope and lateral energy, 2011 (peers).** A hall renders stronger sound with more bass when reflections preserve the temporal envelope; lateral reflections contribute envelopment and distance perception; lateral reflections favourable because of binaural loudness. Source: JASA EL 2011 `[Obs; H]`.

**L3. Disentangling preference, 2012 (peers).** Individual vocabulary profiling; two listener groups with different preferences. Source: JASA 2012 `[Obs; H]`.

**L4. Dynamics and shoebox halls, 2014 (peers).** Lateral reflections emphasise the same frequencies that orchestral forte adds; shoebox halls (up to ~2,200 seats) convey dynamics better; explains their historical success; measurement tour of ten European halls late 2012. Source: PNAS 2014 `[Obs; H]`.

**L5. Musikverein and other shoeboxes, 2015 (peers).** Preference depends on music and on group; even clarity-preferring listeners rank shoeboxes high in forte; Helsinki and Berlin Philharmonie low. Source: IOA 2015 `[Obs; H]`.

**L6. Repertoire, position and taste, 2016; emotional impact (peers).** Six unoccupied halls, 28 assessors; skin-conductance and ratings show stronger emotional impact in Musikverein and Berlin Konzerthaus. Source: JASA 2016; Pätynen and Lokki 2016 `[Obs; H]`.

**L7. Public column, 2019 (public war story).** Linked Kaufmann's Elbphilharmonie incident to Helsinki; said modern vineyard halls lack bass warmth, brilliance and envelopment, that designers "talk of visual intimacy" without stating acoustic goals, that musicians choose the hall shape at the start and the audience is never consulted; aim: get consultants to "not only read but also believe" the research. Source: FMQ Mar 2019 `[SR-g post; H]`. Conflict-of-interest note: critic of a national hall, not its defender.

**L8. Humility result, 2020 (peers).** Listeners find it hard to recognise individual halls across different musical passages; loudness changes how a hall is experienced. Source: Kuusinen and Lokki JASA 2020; Lokki, McLeod, Kuusinen 2020 `[Obs; H]`.

**L9. DIN 18041 absorption placement is misleading, 2023 (peers).** Web-MUSHRA pairwise test distributed to consultants across Europe on their own headphones; the standard's placement guidance did not hold perceptually. Source: Lokki and Kuusinen, IOA 2023 `[Obs; M]`.

**Failure/near-miss:** L8 limits L4–L7's reach; method limits (unoccupied halls, no binaural in Helsinki, Finnish assessors) stated in his own papers.

### 3.6 Option-ranking record (Phase 4 test set): five decision points that recur

**DP1 — The brief wants more size or seats than the acoustics can carry.** Options: (a) refuse and leave; (b) accept and compensate; (c) fix the acoustic dimensions first, then fit the programme; (d) make the client choose with evidence in hand.
- Beranek 1959: objected, stayed, compensated with clouds; outcome failed; disavowed later. Rationale (post): "forces beyond my control". `[SR-i post]`
- Toyota 2003: pushed for ≤2,000; Gehry's method (c): fix maximum seat distance, iterate shape, fit seats → 2,265. `[SR-i post]`
- Marshall 1966: none of the competition entries was parallel-sided; chose the most promising and invented lateral reflectors (b+c). `[Peer post]`
- D'Antonio 1983: a small room cannot have a hall's ITDG; redefined the goal to a reflection-free zone (c). `[SR-i post]`
- Lokki 2019: argues (d), the audience should be consulted and evidence should decide. `[SR-g]`

**DP2 — Which measure to trust when predicting what listeners will hear.** Options: RT alone; ISO 3382 parameter set; a direct-sound measure (D/R, LOC); blind sensory profiles; a physical model.
- Beranek: ITDG then (2008) EDT; never RT alone. Toyota: "not only the numbers" → 1:10 model with felt dolls. Griesinger: D/R above 700 Hz, time to reverberation build-up, LOC. Lokki: blind listening with individual vocabulary; parameters afterwards. D'Antonio/Cox: measured coefficients (absorption and diffusion) over catalogue values.

**DP3 — Published material data disagrees with reality.** Options: use it anyway; correct with a fudge factor; derive from built rooms; measure yourself; standardise.
- Beranek derived audience absorption from 40 then 20 built halls. D'Antonio measured in gyms, built a goniometer, drove a standard. Vercammen (source) corrected labs against a reference absorber. Toyota substitutes a physical model for material arithmetic. Lokki measures the hall itself.

**DP4 — Performers or critics complain after opening or after a change.** Options: defend; re-tune immediately; wait and observe; test blind; publish.
- Beranek 1962–63: proposed a $60k cloud fix, then disavowed the committee's plan. Toyota: ask musicians to play softer, bring in visiting orchestras, expect 3–4 years of adaptation. Griesinger/Barbar 2003: return and re-tune within ten days. Lokki: measure, test blind, publish; the 1992 Avery Fisher jury test by Jaffe and Scarbrough (binaural heads, A/B by musicians) is the method the typical performer skips.

**DP5 — Early reflections: add, remove, or redirect?** Options by context.
- Add lateral/early (<20–50 ms): Beranek (Tanglewood, TOC), Marshall (Christchurch), Lokki (shoebox lateral), Sydney 2022 (Müller-BBM petals). Remove early specular at the listener: D'Antonio (RFZ for mixing), Griesinger (small halls, rear seats, Williams Hall curtain). Toole/Olive (literature): lateral reflections tolerated or preferred in living rooms; King, Leonard and Sikora: no main effect of treatment on mixing. The variable is context: room size, listener distance vs critical distance, task (mixing vs listening), and position.

### 3.8 Breakdown sheets (steps → key points → reasons); `[Inf]` where not in their words

**P1. Predict whether a geometry or material change is audible at a listening position.**
1. Classify the regime: below the Schroeder frequency (modes, room size vs wavelength) or above (reflections, decay). *Key point:* small rooms are modal to ~200–300 Hz; large rooms are geometric nearly everywhere. *Reason:* the governing physics differs, so the audible consequence differs. `[Inf from D'Antonio, Toole literature]`
2. Compute the parameter deltas: RT/EDT, G, C80, ITDG, early lateral fraction, bass ratio, D/R at the position. *Key point:* position-specific, not room-average. *Reason:* Beranek's Arie Crown lesson and Griesinger's few-good-seats finding. `[SR-i]`
3. Compare each delta to its JND (RT and EDT ~5%, G and C80 ~1 dB, LF ~0.05, IACC ~0.075). *Key point:* below 1 JND is inaudible by definition; 1–2 JND is a test case. *Reason:* ISO 3382-1 Annex A; Seat 1 owns refinements. `[Peer]`
4. Check the inputs' uncertainty (coefficient spread). *Key point:* a delta smaller than the input uncertainty is noise. *Reason:* Vercammen. `[Peer]`
5. Write the claim as a falsifiable sentence and hand it to a blind test. *Key point:* name the reference and the listening position. *Reason:* Lokki; Scarbrough 1992. `[Obs]`

**P2. Audit a material coefficient before it enters the library.**
1. Provenance: which lab, which standard, which sample size and mounting. *Reason:* edge effect and mounting dominate low-frequency results. `[Peer]`
2. Cross-source: at least two independent sources; prefer standards-body or academic tables over a single manufacturer. *Reason:* "shopping". `[Peer]`
3. Clamp and flag: Sabine coefficients above 1.0 are allowed in the Sabine formula but flagged; Eyring coefficients never exceed 1 (Beranek 2006). *Reason:* the two equations take different coefficients. `[Obs]`
4. Attach an uncertainty band per octave (wider below 500 Hz). *Reason:* round-robin spread is largest there. `[Peer]`
5. Treat people and seats as materials by area. *Reason:* Beranek 1959/2006. `[Obs]`

**P3. Validate a preset ("room type") against reality.**
1. State the claim: type, volume, occupancy, reference listening position. 2. Pick a measured reference (a public IR database or published hall data: occupied RT, EDT, G, C80, ITDG, bass ratio). 3. Match the preset's parameters within JND of the reference at that position. 4. Run a blind A/B against the reference IR (Seat 1 protocol). 5. Log the deviations and the repertoire used; note that hall identity is weak across repertoire (Lokki 2020), so test with two excerpts. `[Inf from L8]`

**P4. Diagnose a complaint about a room or preset.**
1. Separate adaptation from fault: did anything change; who complains (performers vs listeners); how long since the change (Toyota). 2. Measure before theorising (Beranek's day in the empty hall; Scarbrough's binaural heads). 3. Check bass strength at 125–250 Hz vs 500–1k (Avery Fisher), early-reflection geometry (Arie Crown), D/R and early lateral at the position (Griesinger), occupancy. 4. Make the smallest reversible change and re-test blind (Williams Hall curtain). `[SR-i, Peer]`

### 5.6 Novelty test (your situation)
