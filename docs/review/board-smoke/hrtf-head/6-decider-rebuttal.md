### R6 / Q1 — Items 4–5: weighting and flatness rule (H2 NOT CHECKABLE; H3 CONDITIONS VIOLATED unless weighted). Stated.

**Weighting — yes, solid-angle (Voronoi) weighted, for both averages, on every option.** Exact wording for items 4–5:

> The converter's diffuse-field averages — the DF-EQ curve (scope 3) and the tail-coherence target, i.e. the diffuse-field average of the interaural cross-spectrum (scope 5) — are computed over the **stored** direction subset with each direction weighted by the solid angle of its spherical Voronoi cell, w_i = Ω_i / 4π (the Voronoi cells are the dual of the spherical Delaunay triangulation the converter already computes and stores, scope 3). Both are computed per ear (the DF curve) / per band (the coherence target) in one-third-octave bands, stored in the `.bin` header beside the weights' sum check (Σ w_i = 1), and reported by `--diagnostics` together with the unweighted curve, so the bias the weighting removes is visible on the pinned file.

Whose words: Seat 2 §6 "the DF-EQ and coherence averages are solid-angle (Voronoi) weighted with the cap reported in the header"; Seat 3 §6(ii) "solid-angle-weighted DF average and coherence target". Both gave it as a condition on (c); carrying it to every option is **my wording judgement**, grounded in the corpus definition and not in any seat's rating: Blauert Ch. 4 "Theile's solution", line 525: diffuse-field equalised means "its average TF over all directions = 1"; Begault Ch. 4 "Equalization of HRTFs", line 1489: the average HRTF is for "random" incidence in third-octave bands; Vorländer line 4004: diffuse-field headphones ≡ "random incidence of incoherent waves". Derivation (D1-L15): "over all directions" is (1/4π)∫|H(Ω)|² dΩ; its quadrature on a finite grid is Σ w_i |H_i|² with w_i = Ω_i/4π; an unweighted mean equals it only when every Ω_i is equal, which the SADIE grid is not (F11: 1° azimuth on the 0° ring, 23 elevation rows of differing counts, one point at −90°), and which item 4's subset (≤ ~3000 directions keeping the 0° ring and the −81° row) is not either. I do not claim the sign or size of the unweighted bias; `--diagnostics` measures it at pin time. No cell moves: Seats 2 and 3 named this as their own cure, Seat 1's condition is met by it (below), and it is converter-time work (Seat 5's runtime and undo statements untouched; the header grows by two short curves). If Seat 2 or 3 objects to the generalisation, a second round settles it.

**Rule — the computed weighted curve is always applied; the tolerance decides the header's record of the source state, not whether to apply.** Exact wording for item 5:

> The converter applies its own weighted diffuse-field curve to every set it converts (per ear). The header records (i) the source's EQ state as documented by the publisher (SADIE II: diffuse-field equalised by York, F18), (ii) the converter's computed curve, and (iii) the post-EQ check: the weighted one-third-octave average of the converted set must be 0 dB within 0.1 dB in every band (an automated test; a numerical identity bound, one tenth of the level jnd). **Source-state consistency check (pin time):** if the computed curve on the pinned `D1_HRIR_SOFA.zip` lies within ±1 dB in every one-third-octave band with centre from 200 Hz to 8 kHz, the header records "source: diffuse-field equalised (publisher), converter residual ≤ 1 dB" and F18 is confirmed; if any band exceeds ±1 dB, the header records "source EQ state: not confirmed", the dossier's F18 is marked contradicted, and the phase raises it with Maisara and through the interrogator before the Phase 08 goldens are frozen. The curve above 8 kHz is reported, not gated. Nothing in the rule switches the application off.

Why "always apply" satisfies Seat 1 §6 "applies DF-EQ exactly once" and does not commit the fault of Seat 1 §3 ("a second DF-EQ on already-equalised data is a tone-colour fault"): the fault is applying a curve that is not the set's own diffuse-field average (an external standard curve, or a biased unweighted average); a curve computed from the set itself under the Blauert definition is self-normalising (Seat 3 §5: "it matters little for this EQ, which is self-normalising"; Seat 4 §6: "self-correcting for already-equalised sets"): on a set already equalised to that definition it is ≈ 0 dB and the converted set carries exactly one diffuse-field normalisation — the data-defined one — which the post-EQ test checks. "Exactly once" is thereby the checkable property "weighted average = 1 after the converter" (Blauert line 525), plus the header's record of the source state (Seat 1 §6, first half).

Provenance of the numbers (all **decider's product judgement for a header record; not load-bearing for the head choice**): 1 dB = level jnd, Vorländer Table 6.1 (line 2060, "Level | G | 1 dB"; Seat 1 §2 used the same jnd for the energy gate); one-third-octave bands: Rumsey §3.2.4 line 954 ("near-flat in one-third-octave bands measured in a diffuse field"), Begault line 1489; 200 Hz lower edge: the set's measured range (F26: sweeps 200 Hz–24 kHz, LF modelled below; F18: flat below ~400 Hz by York's model); 8 kHz upper edge: the top of the product's fixed band set `contract::kOctaveBandsHz` (CLAUDE.md) and of the AC 3 ILD band (2–8 kHz, phase-08); 0.1 dB: a numerical bound one tenth of the jnd. Begault's "±1.5–2.5 dB" (line 1489) is the size of the diffuse-minus-free-field difference and is **not** used as a tolerance. These values may be reset by the interrogator at pin time; none of them changes which head wins. **H2** is thereby turned from an uncheckable expectation into a pin-time measurement with a stated rule; **H3** is removed by the weighting.
