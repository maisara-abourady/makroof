Why KU100 over KEMAR, direction only, magnitude unknown: the residual the target listener hears first is tone colour (T4, register High). Shoulder and torso reflections "differ considerably between individuals: a confusing cue unless matched" (Rumsey §3.2.4); the KU100 is the one head in the corpus designed for timbral compatibility on headphones and loudspeakers, near Theile's diffuse-field response, with symmetric pinnae placed to avoid "artificial elevations" (Begault Ch. 4 dummy heads; Rumsey §3.2.4). The torso's benefit is an elevation cue (Roginska & Geluso Ch. 4, "missing shoulders may weaken elevation cues"), but under AX3 the direct sound is frontal and static elevation through non-individual HRTFs is poorly judged anyway (Begault Fig. 2.29: 17° upward bias, scattered). Elevation then matters mostly for floor/ceiling reflections, where listeners tolerate spatial order 4 to 5 against 9 to 13 for the direct sound (Lübeck, Arend, Pörschmann 2022, register Medium-high). So the torso buys a cue the target listener barely uses and risks one they hear first. `[Inferred]`; a controlled KU100-vs-KEMAR study would settle it (R8).

**Is the difference audible at all?** (i) Front/back: with static non-individual HRTFs, reversals run 31% (noise, Wenzel et al. 1993) to 37–47% (speech, Begault & Wenzel 1993), corpus Table 2.3 and Fig. 2.32; Vorländer §6.3.2: "a rather large portion" of subjects report front–back confusion with standard dummy heads. This floor belongs to the untracked generic condition, not to the head. I predict no head on the table moves it detectably for the target listener `[Inferred]`. (ii) Externalisation: head tracking and reverberation, not the HRTF's individuality, moved externalisation (register, Begault/Wenzel/Anderson 2001; corpus: reverberation cut in-head localisation from 25% to ~3%, Begault Ch. 3 Table 3.2). The room is in the render, so the head choice is third-order here. (iii) Colouration: the one cell where I expect a trained-listener ABX difference; whether a naive producer hears it with the room present is untested (asymmetry rule). **Bar: plausibility** (T3, T6), since no real reference is present in a DAW. Exception: if a reference BRIR set exists measured with one of these heads (Seat 2/9, R10), that head alone opens an authenticity path for B9; useful for diagnosis, not the product bar.

**Gates.** AC 3 (ITD 0.6–0.75 ms at ±90°, ILD > 6 dB over 2–8 kHz) is a pipeline sanity check (sign, channel order, onset extraction), not a perceptual gate: the head's ITD is whatever the head produced, and the gate cannot change what the listener hears. The window is sized for a human-like head (Blauert Ch. 2 Fig. 2.30, ≈650 µs at 90°); head size sets ITD (Begault Ch. 4: "probably a major factor"), so this gate does depend on the head; if a set falls outside, challenge the window on R6 evidence, never loosen to pass. ILD > 6 dB and the straight-ahead |ITD| < 20 µs are head-size-robust. The 1° continuity gate (≤ 1 dB, ≤ 30 µs) is a glitch bound: the natural ITD slope near the front is of the order of 10 µs per degree (650 µs over 90°, cosine-weighted; my arithmetic), so a 30 µs step is about three times the natural slope: it catches interpolation faults, not audibility. The perceptual smoothness gate (S7) is the pink-noise azimuth sweep in the listening pack, listened to for steps. Energy ±1 dB is a perceptual gate (level jnd ≈ 1 dB, Vorländer Table 6.1). Tail coherence ±0.1: plausibly perceptual, JND not re-read, `[Hyp]`.
